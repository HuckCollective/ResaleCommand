# The Huckleberry Collective
## Worker Cooperative & Partner Collaboration Charter
*A Democratic, Transparent, and Shared-Equity Economic Engine*

---

### **1. Executive Summary: What is the Huckleberry Collective?**

The **Huckleberry Collective** is an independent **Worker Self-Directed Enterprise (WSDE)** and cooperative retail consortium operating in Portland, Oregon. 

Instead of individual sellers struggling alone with high commercial booth rents, fragmented inventory tracking, and opaque consignor relationships, the Collective creates a **shared commons**:
- **Shared Prime Retail Booths**: Multi-channel physical presence (such as **Memory Den Booth #0EJ** in Portland, antique malls, and curated pop-ups).
- **Shared Professional Infrastructure**: Commercial direct-thermal barcode printing, live online cataloging, automated Ricochet POS syncing, and multi-channel dropcasting.
- **100% Financial Transparency**: Every sale, receipt, fee, and dollar is tracked on an open, auditable digital ledger.
- **Democratic Governance**: Members and partners work as equals. We operate on a **"Democracy at Work"** model where surplus is shared equitably based on the actual goods and labor contributed.

### **2. Why Partner With Us? The Collaborative Advantage**

Running a solo retail antique booth or vintage pop-up is hard:
- Monthly booth rents ($300 – $800/month) eat your margins if you have a slow week.
- You have to do all the sourcing, thermal tagging, shelf staging, dusting, and inventory audits yourself.
- Most consignment shops charge steep 30%–50% fees and provide little visibility into what sold and when.

#### **The Collective Solution:**
1. **Dramatically Lower Overhead**: Shared rent and supplies mean you only pay your proportional share of the physical space you use.
2. **Automated Barcode & Sales Tracking**: Your items are tagged with your own dedicated barcode prefix (e.g. `PDXGL-` for **PDX Game Library**).
3. **Discrete, Protected Ledgers**: Your inventory and payouts are never scrambled with anyone else's. When an item rings up at the register, our software automatically matches the sale to your account.
4. **100% Cost Recovery First**: When your items sell, your initial out-of-pocket inventory cost is returned to you **dollar-for-dollar** before surplus is calculated.
5. **Purchaser Curation Rewards**: The member who purchases/curates the item earns the majority of the net profit on that sale (50% sales patronage dividend).
6. **Surplus Dividends**: Operating profit is distributed democratically—not hoarded by an owner or middleman.

### **4. Monthly Settlement Cadence & Booth Rent Accounting**

#### A. The Monthly Payout Reality
Memory Den (and most antique malls) pays **once a month** by check or direct deposit. Memory Den automatically deducts their 15% register commission and the **$382.50 fixed booth rent** directly off the top before delivering the net payout.

Because of this, cooperative settlements are calculated **monthly**, matching the venue payout report:

$$\text{Net Check from Venue} = \text{Gross Register Sales} - \text{15\% Venue Fee} - \text{\$382.50 Booth Rent}$$

Under our **70 / 30 rule**, the collective's 30% take naturally covers the $382.50 booth rent without individual rent assessments:
- When monthly booth sales reach $2,500, the 30% pool generates **$472.50**, which easily pays the $382.50 rent, software costs, and leaves a reserve buffer!
- Slower months are cushioned by the accumulated reserve pool so no member is hit with a surprise bill.

### **5. The Purchaser Reward: Why Curators Take Home the Most Pay**

When an item sells, **the person who purchased and curated that item receives the vast majority of the money**.

#### Real Dollar Example: $100 Sale at Memory Den
- **Member A** bought a vintage coat for **$20.00**.
- **Member B** tagged it, staged it at Memory Den, and posted a photo.
- Coat sells for **$100.00**:
  - Memory Den 15% fee: -$15.00 $\rightarrow$ **$85.00 net deposit**
  - Member A gets purchase cost back 100%: -$20.00 (Direct to Member A)
  - **Net Surplus Generated**: **$65.00**
    - **70% Curator Profit**: **+$45.50 (Direct to Member A!)**
    - **30% Commons Pool**: **$19.50** (Covers booth rent, labels, software, & labor)
- **Member A takes home $65.50 on a $20 purchase (65.5% of gross sale).**
- **The Collective earns $19.50 toward the $382.50 rent and staffing.**

### **6. Legal Structure: The Cooperative Consortium Model**

A common question is: *"Should every member be an owner of the LLC?"*

#### Why We Use the Cooperative Consortium (Contractual Membership)
**Huckleberry Collective LLC** operates as the clean legal and operational anchor (holding the Memory Den booth lease, business insurance, bank account, and commercial software tools).

All collaborators join as **Cooperative Associate Members (Consortium Partners)** governed by this Charter:

| Factor | Consortium Model (Our Standard) | Adding Everyone to the LLC |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tax Filings** | **Simple.** Single Schedule C for the LLC. Partners receive standard **Form 1099-NEC** for net earnings over $600. Partners file their own Schedule C. | **Nightmare.** Mandatory IRS Form 1065 (Partnership Return) and Schedule K-1s. CPA cost is $1,000–$2,500/year. |
| **Partner Autonomy** | Partners maintain their own identity and brand (e.g. `PDXGL-` for PDX Game Library). | Partners are legally merged into one company. |
| **Exit Simplicity** | Clean and fluid. A partner can exit anytime, take their inventory, and receive their final check with zero legal paperwork. | Requires formal buyout agreements, state legal amendments, and corporate asset valuation. |
| **Democracy** | **1 Member = 1 Vote** guaranteed contractually by this Charter. | Governed by statutory corporate stock votes. |

#### The 1-Year Candidacy Path
If a dedicated worker proves over 12 consecutive months that they are committed as a permanent core co-founder, the collective can vote by unanimous consent to formally amend the LLC Operating Agreement and add them as an equity Member with the State of Oregon.

### **7. Marketing, Photography & Social Media Labor Standards**

In retail antique booths, items do not sell unless foot traffic enters the aisle. **Marketing and social media are recognized as essential cooperative labor.**

#### A. What Qualifies as Marketing Labor
- Photographing new intake and booth displays.
- Drafting and publishing Dropcasts in the `/social` studio.
- Filming and editing video Reels/TikToks.
- Designing physical booth signage and shelf tags.
- Answering customer inquiries and arranging front-desk holds at Memory Den.

#### B. How Marketing Labor is Gauged (Deliverable Standard)
To ensure complete fairness and prevent unverified hours, marketing time is logged against **tangible deliverables**:
1. **Dropcast Record**: Every published broadcast in `/social` has a timestamp, item list, and unique ID.
2. **Before & After Photos**: Booth restyling hours are verified with timestamped shelf photos.
3. **Weekly Labor Allocation**: Members agree to standard allocations (e.g. 2 to 4 hours per week for social media and staging).

All verified marketing hours earn **Tier 3 Labor Patronage Dividends** on par with physical tagging and hauling.

Each member or partner organization owns a dedicated, collision-free barcode prefix:

- **`PDXGL-`**: PDX Game Library curated tabletop board games, card games, and expansions.

When venue payout CSVs (Ricochet POS) are uploaded, Resale Command partitions sales automatically by prefix with zero cross-contamination.

Major operational decisions (new retail leases, buying major equipment, onboarding new partners) use the **4-Signal Consent Protocol**:

| Signal | Meaning | Actionable Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 🟢 **Agree** | "I support this proposal." | Counted toward positive consensus. |
| 🟡 **Abstain** | "I trust the collective to proceed without my input." | Neutral; does not block progress. |
